# Galaxy Z Fold7 gameplay review

**Native gameplay-camera critic: 9.5/10 (pass).** Source `4a995122a2221de0d2caf299d140a7df25641eef`, scene `res://field_opening.tscn`. I scored screen fill, fold/rotate framing, touch/pinch, HUD, hero visibility, gate access, running, orbit contacts, pursuit, and hit/recovery one point each. The visible 2× cutaway edge costs half the visibility point. Physical Fold7 acceptance is pending.

I reviewed the cover and inner layouts at the gameplay camera, the stocked and early HUD states, and matched before/after views of Character 1 behind a pine, a mining bank and the front fence. The rendering and controller evidence comes from Godot. It covers the Fold7 screen dimensions in a desktop renderer. A physical handset run, Android gesture/cutout behavior, and device frame pacing remain open.

| Check | Observation |
| --- | --- |
| Screen fill and proportions | Native 1080 × 2520 and 1968 × 2184 frames, both landscape rotations and the fold-back case fill the window. Character 1 stays at the center. The cover keeps its larger vertical field of view. I inspected both 1× and 2× south-gate captures and their in-engine two-finger gesture records. The previous capture had 49 almost entirely dark neutral rows. Both new portrait frames have zero (over 99% of the row below RGB 100 with channel spread under 2). |
| HUD and touch | The portrait and live HP, item badges, workshop inventory and objective are legible against snow. The first review found white title letters falling outside the portrait plate. I verified the corrected title, the separate −4 feedback, and the first objective remaining visible during threat. The quality control retains a compact face over an enlarged 82-unit touch target. |
| Obstructed character | Matched pine, stone and front-fence comparisons expose the head, torso, hands and boots at 1× and 2×. The resources remain recognizable. The normal movement route recorded 168 pine and 34 quarry occlusion samples with zero missed activations. On a close fence or rock, the stippled edge of the cutaway is visible and can make the feet momentarily appear on top of the obstruction. |
| Camp access and run | All 12 crossings passed across two gates, two directions and three perimeters without leaf contact. Short and full drags both reached the run animation at 2.80 m/s. Jitter stayed idle and releasing the drag returned to rest. |
| Orbit | Swept geometry checks covered the shaft interior, tip and outside-limit misses. Moving contacts repeated at 30, 60 and 120 simulation steps per second. The measured base axe tip was 2.70 m from its pivot, and the upgraded axe tip was 3.26 m. Simulation tick rate is distinct from phone frame rate. |
| Threats and feedback | With the orbit active, a naturally generated pair of wolves approached from a nearest starting distance of 6.79 m and bit in 2.20 s. Regular contact reduced HP from 30 to 28, and a separate alpha contact reduced it to 26. The HUD loss, directional recoil and flash responded. Camp safety, old damaged/dead saves and both recovery destinations passed. |
| Regression route | I replayed the existing settlement route at 60 simulation steps per second in a disposable paid-camp profile. It completed in 38.12 simulated seconds. A separate earlier unpaced headless process crashed before it reached the first gate. Paced 16- and 60-step runs completed. The crash happened in the Linux headless harness; the phone behavior is unmeasured. |

The combat changes and their tests are my work. I reviewed the separate camera, HUD, input, gate, orbit and visibility changes as a second reviewer.

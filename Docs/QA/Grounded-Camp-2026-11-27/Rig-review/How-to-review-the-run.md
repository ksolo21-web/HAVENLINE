# How to review Character 1 in motion

Use the saved Character 1 design with the mittens, short legs, thick boots and two distinct metal studs on each outer boot. The recordings document how the blue-coated player reads from above. Source A shows the approach to equipment at 12.00–12.40 seconds. Source B shows the lateral run toward the weapon purchase at 13.00–13.50 seconds. Measure the motion of the ground against the moving body's hood-to-sole height. The carried pile obscures the far foot. On A at 12.30 seconds the body and its dark floor contact are visibly separated. These 10 Hz source cuts support a brief flight phase. They cannot establish the airborne percentage, a distance in centimetres, or an exact joint pose. In Godot, start the field scene through its ordinary entry point. Use a real, disposable profile directory. A symlink triggers the production save guard. Check that the field is ready and persistence is available, move by ordinary touch input, and record the body position, active gear, actual pose, footprint count and full gameplay image. Repeat at 1× and 2×. Measure motion in metres against the rendered snow surface. The raised clothing and carried timber make a fixed camera crop unreliable for comparing character speed.

Measure each boot's original sole vertices at 120 Hz with its actual imported skin transforms. Track the heel, ball and toe through support transfer. Compare the world positions of the same material patch during support. Also count how long both soles stay above the floor, and how high they rise together. Build 6 could hold a material point nearly still on the snow while keeping *both* boots above 1 cm for 62.37% of steady motion. With a 0.736 m boot sweep and 7.0 m/s travel, its available planted time per step was only about 0.105 s. Examine support duration and slip together. Check the boot's construction at actual stance and swing extremes. A frame seen from above can hide a collapsed leather upper. Retain the same outer stud triangles across revisions, including all vertices backing the hardware. On the original boot, the two studs are round and the ankle strap has visible thickness. In a rejected revision the left and right upper studs narrowed to 19% and 15% of their rest width. Render each exact surface from both sides, compare its in-plane deformation over the loop, and inspect the leather, cuff and calf surrounding it. Also check actual terrain contact after changing skinning or bending the ankle.

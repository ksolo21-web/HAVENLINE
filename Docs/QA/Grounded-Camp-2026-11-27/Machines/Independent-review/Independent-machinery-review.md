# Independent paid machinery review

I reviewed the final native models in the shipping Level 1 scene at its default zoom and its 2× pinch limit. Each workstation received material through the usual nearby transfer. The test used a copy of an already paid disposable profile and staged the hero and one carried lot for each case. I compared the game against the three Havenline equipment turnarounds. The locked A footage supplied the purchase, material travel and output sequence.

| Check | Observation |
|---|---|
| Pay before building | A protected eight-purchase save reopened with all three stations absent. The touch route bought the cooker, saw and quarry separately, in that order. Each completed only on the final resource receipt. The hero cleared the full-size collider at all three reveals and could move away. The source also shows the plot filling and a brief upward light as the machine appears. |
| Placement | The north machinery row keeps the cooking, sawing and mining operations inside camp. The final north-wall-to-quarry clearance is 0.75 m. The west-wall-to-saw clearance is 0.76 m. The hero is 0.62 m wide. A separate route probe swept a 0.96 m diameter through the service aisle to all three loading positions, the storehouse, the counter and both gates. These two narrower side clearances are single-person spaces. |
| Saw | Its golden timber, clamp, toothed disc, timber legs and metal brackets reproduce the shop form. The final right cut end measured median sRGB [201,135,83] against [202,135,85] on the artwork. The arbor and workpiece move, and a plank appears in the outfeed. |
| Cooker | The snow cover, blue chamber, front drive, side vessel and two bins are recognizable. Meat enters at the left, the working chamber warms and the cooked portion reaches the right bin. A three-cut feed left two distinct raw models waiting while one cooked. The exhaust was active. Its pale smoke has low contrast against fence snow. |
| Quarry | The lattice derrick, guy cable, winch, tracked cab and rock base reproduce the sheet's silhouette. Its winch rotates under power. The spindle travels 0.54 m, fine stone cuttings emerge from the foot during contact and the output tray receives a block. The front hero angle partially overlaps the mast. The cutting cue remains visible alongside him. |
| Evidence limits | The Linux captures were 630 × 800 pixels. The current review covers graphics and playability at these two game-camera settings. Handset presentation, 4K Ultra performance and response at 60–120 Hz require separate evidence. Reference A shows a fishing line and a crop line. The saw, quarry and meat cooker are Havenline’s adaptations. The footage does not define exact prices or cycle durations for them. |

## Independent grades

| Area | Grade | Evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Separate purchasing and entrance animation | 9.5 / 10 | Each touch purchase settles before construction. Light rises through the shape during the spring reveal. The player can walk away, and the earned station reopens on the next launch. |
| Front work aisle and safe clearance | 9.6 / 10 | The actor clears the full-size bodies, and each station connects to service, storage and both gate paths. |
| Timber saw appearance and cycle | 9.6 / 10 | Its shape, cut face and clamp now match the render closely. The log and blade move, and the plank reaches the rack. |
| Meat cooker appearance and cycle | 9.5 / 10 | Queued meat, working heat and a finished roast are legible at gameplay zoom. The pale exhaust can still blend into snow. |
| Quarry appearance and cycle | 9.5 / 10 | The tower, winch and tracked power unit match the artwork. The loaded spindle visibly discharges short-lived stone powder and chips at its base. The ordinary-camera front and west approaches showed contact and a settled finished block. |

The lowest score was 9.5. All five machinery criteria passed this desktop gameplay review. Physical phone performance and the owner’s final art judgment remain open.

In both isolated camera trials, all three systems accepted the raw lot on tick 10. The saw and quarry posted the output on tick 98, and the cooker on tick 110. At 60 physics ticks per second this is 1.47 s and 1.67 s between the recorded receipts. Their configured work intervals are 1.45 s and 1.65 s. Each produced exactly one output, with distinct spending, start and completion events.

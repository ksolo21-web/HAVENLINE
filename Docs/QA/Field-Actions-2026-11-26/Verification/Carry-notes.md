# Character 1’s back load

The rack has two rows of fixed positions. Timber fills two positions per tier. Stone, meat, slate, coal and metal ore each have their own vertical stack. Picking up another material or emptying its stack leaves the other packets in place. The timber supports adjust to the width of the seated packets. A small camera-facing opening in worn materials keeps the head visible from behind tall loads.

The nine- and twenty-bundle captures used the Level 1 gameplay camera at 1× in separate controlled poses at a Fold cover resolution of 1080 × 2520. All nine or twenty native meshes were present in every corresponding pose. Each token passed through the game’s pickup and transfer functions. The four stills show the settled load. The isolated tests exercised all 63 nonempty combinations of material kinds and 20 viewport, zoom and load cases. They removed each material in turn and checked that every other kind kept its position. A separate transaction run earned three of each kind, deposited one from each stack, and restored the remaining two of each at their original transforms. The largest inventory cases capped the number of displayed packets to preserve headroom and retained the logical balances. Two sloping braces secure each occupied shelf to the belt-level cross timber. Each brace reuses the original shelf crosspiece’s beveled geometry and vertex paint. A real triangle check follows the brace centerline from the inserted end toward its midpoint. Each end must intersect a frame or shelf face at least 2 mm inside the joint and within 105 mm of the end. The minimum was 4.028 mm across 1,728 end checks in 252 material/count combinations. Empty rows retract their supports. The first row in the comparison shows a control with only the braces hidden.

# Moving paid camp equipment

Open **Move** after founding the camp. Tap a building, then tap or drag to a new spot. The translucent model previews the destination. The solid outline follows its footing, and the dashed amber outline marks the area needed for loading or upgrades. **Place here** confirms an available destination. **Reselect** discards the preview, and **Done** returns to the gameplay camera with the previous pinch setting.

The furnace, storehouse, cooker, saw bench, quarry drill and watchtower move with their collision bodies and working parts. The counter can move along the north fence. Its service side faces camp, and waiting customers walk to the new queue. The watchtower stays on the eastern threat side. Before saving, the game checks for occupied ground and verifies a walking route to both gates and each working side. The perimeter, rescue landing and future paid plots retain clear space.

The replay bought the cooker using the ordinary joystick and purchase plot with 120 wood and 80 stone in the isolated inventory. It moved the cooker by touch and fed it at the new position. A customer then paid for the cooked meal across the relocated counter. A second interaction deposited meat at the relocated storehouse. Later workshop receipts and other prerequisites were staged to cover the complete layout in one session. A separate starter-house run used the relocated upgrade mat to reach the reinforced tier. An independent cold process loaded all seven relocated objects.

The seven equipment placements preserve their original facing. The map interaction and footprint highlights are an owner-requested design addition. Locked recording B at 5.4–6.6 seconds supplies the original purchased-camp scale, gateway clearance and fixed gameplay view. The workshop meshes and their silhouettes follow the archived cooker, saw and quarry sheets. [Watch the native 4.75-second touch sequence](Move-paid-cooker-by-touch.mp4). It shows selecting the paid cooker, checking the red blocked destination, confirming the green destination and returning to the gameplay view. The capture sampled the native renderer at 12 frames per second with the simulation running at 60 physics ticks per second.

The native captures use Godot 4.7.2 with Mesa llvmpipe and a 504 × 892 portrait viewport. Physical Fold 7 touch and device performance require separate measurement.

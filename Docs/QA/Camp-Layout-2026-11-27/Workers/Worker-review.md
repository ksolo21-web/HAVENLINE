The working forester uses the scene’s existing model with separate thigh, shin, ankle and toe bones. Independent gameplay sampling after rescue captured 10.55 m of travel at 60 Hz and a median forward speed of 6.24999 m/s during the measured run, matching Character 1’s 6.25 m/s top speed. Slower approaches, turns and final steps blend down from that pace. The gait was compared with the separate cyan-clad worker running to the fish counter in the supplemental Whiteout Survival ad at 28–31 seconds. The original footage is 24 fps. Its figure is small enough that foot contact is best checked against the actual game skin. [Reference shot at normal and one-third speed](../Rig-review/Reference-worker-real-then-third-speed.mp4) · [Independent in-game forester measurements](../Rig-review/worker-locked-haul-head-kinematics.json)

Across 240 native stride samples at full speed, the forester’s measured knee bend rises to 43.5° under load, extends to 14.0° on push-off, folds to 119.0° in recovery and extends to 15.0° before contact. The independent reviewer tracked 1,351 actual boot-surface vertices. Each foot has one support interval per stride. The recovering boot clears the snow by 50–57 mm through the former scuff, reaches roughly 154 mm, and median planted-sole slip is 0.00010 m/s. The worst sustained 4 mm contact patch moves 4.5 mm through the stance, down from 64–68 mm before the final heel-to-toe correction.

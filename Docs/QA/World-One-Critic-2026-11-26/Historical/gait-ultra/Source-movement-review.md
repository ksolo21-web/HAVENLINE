# Source movement and gameplay framing

The strongest retained movement evidence is two short approaches to a payment pad. Both show a blue-coated character carrying goods, followed by a stop and a transfer. The camera follows close enough that the pad and fence travel much farther across the image than the character does. These intervals support a comparison of on-camera travel and stopping. They do not expose two complete, clearly identifiable free-handed stride cycles.

| Observed in the original gameplay pixels | Recording A | Recording B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source and phase | 12.00–12.40 s, diagonal travel toward the fishing upgrade | 13.00–13.50 s, mostly lateral travel into the axe-upgrade pad |
| Movement of fixed ground features | About 75 px left and 58 px down in the detail cell | About 102 px left and 3 px down in the detail cell |
| Movement of the character on screen | Blue-coat median stays within about 5 px in either axis | Blue-coat median stays within about 4 px in either axis |

| Visible scale, hood to boot | About 71 detail-cell px, or 178 original px. About 16.5% of the 1080 px short viewport dimension. | About 43 detail-cell px, or 116 original px. About 10.7% of the short dimension. In B's 6.00 s outdoor view the actor occupies about 12.6%. |

The overview sheets intentionally trim the bottom of the game. Their 1080 × 1800 extraction rectangle makes the torso appear around 53% down. In the full 1080 × approximately 1920 gameplay rectangle, the same character is near vertical center. Five original B frames at 6, 8, 10, 28, and 48 seconds give visible blue-coat medians of 49.8%, 49.4%, 49.8%, 49.6%, and 49.2%. A3 and A16 give 50.6% and 50.0%. Near the gate the moving B14–15.5 overview cells, mapped back to full-game coordinates, give about 48.9–49.9%. Source phone bars above and below gameplay are excluded. Normalizing to actor height excludes cargo. The rates above are two-dimensional, camera-projected travel and depend on direction and recording playback. They do not specify a world speed. The denser tool-orbit sequence in B at 5.40–6.10 s mostly shows a character standing on the pad while a weapon and hostiles move around him.

**Gait and arms.** The boots change silhouette while the loaded character approaches. The held load hides portions of the far leg and arms, and the mitts stay fairly close to the torso. The clearest empty-handed view in the dense A strip is already stationary. The A paid and wheat overviews and the B gate/hostile overview cover longer routes, but sample every 0.5 s. Those intervals can skip entire steps. They do not establish same-foot cycle duration, foot-strike frame, numerical arm pump, a walk-to-run switch, or a distinct sprint animation. The source also provides no verified crouch, jump, or pinch gesture in these sequences. A small frame-to-frame magnification change in A could have several causes. It is insufficient to attribute zoom behavior to an input.

**Provenance.** The published frames come from recording A, SHA-256 `62f7cfe644de3826cfdd6d91bc23c0ab8d9e4fde3e175c90f762e604547bb0ca`, and B, SHA-256 `4c9051cbea6df0efa3e7288b13b6d17da46e857274869b0b43d2f96ff6cc79d2`. The detailed sheets are `A-12.00-13.40-detail-sequence.png` and `B-13.00-14.20-detail-sequence.png`; their extraction manifest records the original frame hashes, crops and nominal seek times. The full source movies were unavailable in the checkout and mounted-file size search. A read-only Git mirror covered 3,586 reachable commits and 8,865 historical paths. No tracked movie or standalone reference-evidence archive appeared. The GitHub release list was empty. The 0.10 s sampling allows the approach/stop comparison above. A supplementary ad or synthetic animation cannot establish missing timing in the locked recordings.

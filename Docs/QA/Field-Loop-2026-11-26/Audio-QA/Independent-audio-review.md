# Field sound and music

I regenerated the field audio in an isolated directory using the versioned synthesis script and the five recovered Havenline inputs. All **46 WAV files** match the game files byte for byte. The five recovered inputs include Alfred’s field score, winter air, hearth crackle, wood pickup and furnace-earned cue. The additional field arrangements and effects derive from the Havenline synthesis code. The camp and danger arrangements use the field score’s 96-BPM timing, with different register, instrumentation and attack. [Rebuild hashes](Independent-bank-rebuild.json) · [Five recovered fingerprints](Alfred-cue-fingerprints.json)

The native game-event log from the earned 53.40-second touch route shows a field score and wind at spawn. The danger layer rises around the wolf engagement, then drops back after the encounter. Camp music and hearth crackle begin near the built furnace. Real material debits, deposits, gate movement, purchase and reveal each start their own effects. A low-priority step, swish or separate drop can yield a voice during overlapping effects. A recorded event and a launched stream alone cannot demonstrate an audible cue in the output.

I independently decoded the trimmed 24 fps, 48 kHz stereo injury-and-recovery movie, aligned each gameplay event to its actual captured video frame, and matched an excerpt of its expected source waveform against the decoded mix. Thirty cue instances exceeded normalized correlation .40. Their estimated starts were 4–55 ms later than the visual frame, with a 13 ms median. Confirmed examples include wolf attack, player hurt and the reset. Thirty-nine additional attempts did not clear the individual-cue confidence threshold during the busy overlap. Their event logs cannot establish independent audibility. This 8.375-second clip uses a documented diagnostic source. [Waveform ledger](Independent-diagnostic-waveforms.json) · [Actual injury and recovery movie](../Gameplay-Evidence/Diagnostic-Wolf-Defeat-Recovery.mp4)

Locked recording A is documented as having no audio stream. Recording B had an AAC track, but its full source audio was unavailable. The recovered image sheets and derived excerpts have no audio. They establish visible action timings, not melodies, timbres, relative instrumental balance or precise source-side sound onset. The verified originality here is the reproducibility and source provenance of the Havenline audio. This assessment uses decoded samples, mix states and time-aligned waveforms; it does not rate loudspeaker playback, preference, or similarity to an unheard track. In the 3.96-second native portrait purchase capture, the first two wood-payment samples matched their event frames within 5.7 and 9.7 ms. The completed purchase cue matched at 2.555 seconds, 13.7 ms after its event frame, and the reveal matched at 2.886 seconds, 11.1 ms after its event frame. The final wood debit shares its frame with the purchase and two construction cues. The audio stream confirms the purchase there, while the individual final wood, build and rise components fall below the independent waveform threshold.

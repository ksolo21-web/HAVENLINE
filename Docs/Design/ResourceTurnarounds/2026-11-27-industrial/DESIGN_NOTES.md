# Resource design notes

These are authored visual briefs for the new concept sheets. They specify legible item identities without creating new economy rules or asserting mechanically certified hardware. The selected-source references are at commit `79973ab632a0916acec9a6ddad7895f1243a4713`.

Current design status: all nine authored briefs are frozen for this concept generation pass and reconciled to the actual prompt files in `prompts/`; exact file hashes are recorded in `RESOURCE_ROSTER.json`. Prompts 01–03 also have explicit second-pass repairs for projection, iron material and the gear's double-sided hub. This freeze records intended forms and review requirements only. It does not approve generated pixels, establish shared native 3D geometry or waive repairs required by independent visual review.

## Common presentation

Use the existing resource-sheet grammar: premium sculpted stylized 3D illustration, a restrained dark navy studio background, clear warm-white labels, four views per row and two rows. Front/right/rear/left views and all four diagonals describe one stable object. Lighting may shift with the view, but object details must rotate with the object rather than being mirrored or exchanged. Printed degrees identify the intended conceptual orbit; they do not prove metric camera calibration.

Use modern bold sans-serif typography. For a symmetric box-like footprint, 45° and 225° share the upper-left-to-lower-right projected long-axis diagonal, while 225° reveals the opposite physical faces. The 135° and 315° views share the opposite diagonal. Similar opposite-view silhouettes are valid for symmetric objects; front/rear markings and side details must still follow the actual physical faces. A repeated 135°/315° orientation under the 225° label is a defect.

Avoid novelty detail that turns an ordinary carried item into an entire workstation or adds implied currencies. Keep major visual landmarks simple enough for the independent critic to track through all eight views.

Each depicted form is a representative visual token for its existing resource identity. One bar, sheet, gear, assembled module or container in an image does not establish a literal inventory unit, weight, volume, recipe yield or cost. The gear does not add a separate gear resource; the mobility module's timber fitting does not add a mounting-sleeper resource; the drum does not add container stock. Existing resource IDs and authored recipes remain unchanged. Iron bars, sheet metal and mechanical parts use one object each, with no bundle or tray.

## Item briefs

| Item | Distinctive form and boundaries |
|---|---|
| Iron bars | One long forged iron bar: a rectangular cuboid about four times as long as its square end section, resting horizontally, with softened/chamfered corners. The r2 refinement requires continuous dark cool charcoal-gray metallic faces, broad smooth satin reflections and tiny shallow forging marks; no coarse fractured stone facets or bronze/copper edging. Maintain its length and square section. No stack, strap, top recess, stamp or trapezoid casting shape. It must differ from the existing squat polished blue-silver steel ingot. The saved resource remains `iron_bars`; raw ore remains `metal`. |
| Sheet metal | One flat rectangular pressed metal sheet, about twice as long as it is wide and about 1/45 of its width in thickness. Light cool silver-gray satin metal, small rounded corners and a narrow readable edge highlight. It rests flat; the elevated camera shows its broad face and thin edge. Front/rear show the long edge, left/right the short edge. No stack, folds, corrugation, holes, bolts, bands or extra plates. |
| Mechanical parts | One upright forged spur gear serves as the token for aggregate mechanical-parts stock. Exactly eight chunky rounded teeth, one circular axle bore and a raised cylindrical central hub projecting equally from both front and rear faces; dark blue-gray body with silver machined tooth edges and bore. Preserve tooth count, the double-sided hub, hole size and thickness. Front/rear show the opposing gear faces; left/right must show the gear thickness between both equal hub projections. No tray, box, extra cogs or loose fasteners. The saved resource remains `mechanical_parts`. |
| Drive assembly | One compact gearbox/transmission: rounded rectangular deep navy housing, one shallow orange service cover on top with four corner bolts, two coaxial short silver shafts with collars on the opposing left/right end walls, and four low mounting feet. Front/rear show the shallow-ribbed long casing walls and side-projecting shafts; left/right face the shaft ends. Preserve the cover on top, two shafts, four feet and four cover bolts. No front-facing shaft, cylinders, wheels or whole engine. |
| Mobility assembly | One axle-and-hub carrier: a horizontal silver-gray axle joining two identical circular hub flanges, each with six bolts; one navy U-shaped mounting bracket; one orange center sleeve; and one warm timber mounting sleeper underneath. Preserve the two flanges, axle, bracket and sleeper through the orbit. Front/rear show the long axle arrangement; left/right show opposing hub faces. No tires, rubber, tracks, propeller, rails or loose modules. `traction_kit` remains the saved ID and Mobility assembly the visible name. This is the shared resource form for wheel/track/rail/marine fittings, not a claim that an identical finished wheel fits every world. |
| Crude oil | One sealed upright dark gunmetal cylindrical drum with two raised reinforcing hoops, rounded top/bottom rims, one closed silver bung near the lid's front edge, and one continuous muted amber-orange center band. The band carries exactly two identical cream drop-symbol labels on opposing front/rear faces; side views show the band without a full facing label. Track the bung on the same physical side as the front label. Preserve the drum, two hoops, one bung and two labels; no handle, X-ribs, spout, spill or pump. Oil is the resource; the drum is its presentation and must differ from the existing orange fuel jerrycan. |
| Stone blocks | One dressed warm-gray masonry block, about 2:1:1 in length/width/height, with soft bevels, small rounded corners, restrained fine mineral grain and smooth tool-worked faces. Front/rear show long faces and left/right square ends. Preserve its regular worked shape; it must remain distinct from rough stone, metal and a thin sheet. No group, stack, rubble, straps or invented brick inventory. |
| Gems | One polished saturated cyan-blue gemstone for `premium_gems`, with a simple broad emerald-style cut: a slightly elongated rectangular footprint with clipped corners, a broad flat octagonal top, one ring of broad crown facets and a shallow lower body resting on a small flat base. Keep the same facet arrangement and proportions across views. No ore matrix, mined cluster, jewelry, additional mining mechanic, price, exchange rate or reward promise. |
| Relay component | One chamfered gray armored cassette with exactly one cyan horizontal slot on the front, one dark connector socket on the rear, matching shallow vent panels on the two sides and one centered top lifting bridge. The bridge has two integral supports, not extra handles. Front obliques show the cyan slot and correct side vent; rear obliques show the connector and correct side vent. Unseen rear/side construction is explicitly authored clarification of the Relay Rover cargo language. No loose cables, minor fastener arrays, duplicated windows or new resource balance. This remains the finite serialized `relay_component` project object. |

# Havenline | Build 13 dynamic gameplay audit
**Scope:** Read-only source and production-plan review. No gameplay, GitHub or task-plan changes; no new runtime playtest. Build 13 remains the signed foundation, not final approval. [Signed receipt][apk] · [Current published plan][plan]

## Keep the good parts of Build 13
The player can already put multiple meals on a counter, collect posted cash, and bank it at the warehouse. Existing physical transfers and the save checkpoint are useful foundations. Build 13 still runs a single-meal loop: one rescued-worker meal at a time or a belt attendant, and payment calculated as one note per served order. Counter geometry, staffing, and presentation are separable from the sale logic: keep the physical counter and visible goods, not a staffed register. Offers are fixed wood/stone/meat entries; an existing comment already protects a price after its first paid contribution. Wolves are wired to the player and retire when too far from the player. That code cannot represent threats continuing at camp while the player explores. ## Priority gaps and how to prove the fixes

| Gap | Design to carry forward | First decisive test |
|---|---|---|
| **P0 · A buyer can only be served through a narrow role and recipe.** | The unlocked survivor bulk-stocks the counter from actual processed inventory, then leaves if another job matters. The player can do the same. Customers use the exterior face, take their own order and leave ordinary money in its own visible stack. The counter displays distinct goods piles, not one ambiguous heap. No spawned server, staffed selling condition or register. Raw materials can still be hauled; do not sell raw by default. | One worker delivers two finished goods types in one trip; the worker leaves. Two buyers take different baskets, leave payment, then the player or worker sweeps and banks the cash. Inspect both sides at gameplay zoom. |
| **P0 · Demand can become impossible even when a resource was ‘unlocked.’** | Use a shared reachability planner for orders, plots and upgrades. Check the route, reachable source, recipes and **full** upstream inputs, required machines/permissions, job capacity and quantities. Distinguish available now, attainable through an independently affordable plot, and impossible now. An attainable-but-unbuilt item can be shown as a future opportunity; it may not block the queue or demand that the plot pay for itself. Missing stock on hand alone is not unreachability. | A quarry exists but its unlock cost needs the quarry’s own output; a cooker was destroyed; a route is blocked; another project holds the last stock. Reject cycles and impossible quantities; let safe orders/purchases continue. |
| **P0 · ‘Dynamic’ can lead to prices that move on the player.** | Change item types and quantities at explicit progression milestones using authored curves, vetted recipe substitutions and a stable per-offer choice. Publish and freeze a quote when it becomes payable, including partial funding and authorized worker jobs. If a path becomes unavailable, pause or offer a clearly labeled reissue and return committed contributions exactly once if canceled. Protect mandatory starter milestones from random economic deadlocks. | Start funding a mixed-resource plot; level up, reload and return after equipment loss. The bill does not silently change or double-charge. A mandatory unlock has an affordable path from an empty inventory. |
| **P0 · Sales, bulk delivery and money can race.** | Record a typed basket and price under one order ID. Reserve complete stock or offer a clearly authored smaller order. Goods become sold and cash becomes collectible at one durable commit; animations follow it. Worker and player use the same custody rules and can never pick up the same cash. Cash stack is a view of money actually at the counter, not ‘orders served minus bank balance.’ Separate stock capacity from rendered stack size. | Duplicate buyer demands, queue exit after reservation, worker/player picking up cash together, full counter, and closing before/after taking goods or dropping cash. Every unit and paid sale accounts for exactly once. |
| **P1 · The survivor needs to finish jobs when conditions change.** | Give owned workers goals with reservations and bounded retries: inspect demand, pick a safe route, load useful mixed quantities, deliver, collect exposed payments, bank earnings, and re-evaluate. Skip empty/full/closed stations without oscillating. Authorized hunts are proactive; combat outside that assignment is defensive. Suspend or redirect work under threat, release reservations on death and reassign once. Always allow the player to run every essential job solo. | Two workers want the last meals while a gate is jammed and cash needs banking. One reroutes, waits for a meaningful change, or does another job. The other never debits reserved stock. Kill the hunter mid-return and verify the chosen cargo disposition once. |
| **P0 · Enemy intent is attached to the player, not the encounter.** | Give hostiles explicit eligible targets, reachable contact and aim telegraphs. By default favor exposed player/pets or a camp assault objective; hunters that engage may be retaliated against. An outside survivor becomes eligible when the player and pets are sheltered. After a warned and sustained move beyond the defense range, enemy intent can widen to exposed survivors, pets and eligible camp targets. Preserve a current readable attack wind-up rather than snapping aim every frame. Never use camera visibility as the targeting or siege trigger. | Repeat with hero/pet outside, both sheltered, survivor hunting, hero far, and the hero returning around the outer edge. Check what the enemy chose, why, and that only legal contact damages the target. |
| **P0 · Destruction and replacement can strand progression.** | Give fence/gate segments high HP, localized breach/collision states and physical repair plots. Damage repair prices use material, missing HP and severity; commit a fixed estimate and re-quote visibly if additional damage must change the bill. A destroyed machine must be bought again at Level 1. Explicitly settle its input/output cargo, in-flight goods, active orders and job reservations. Define which camp assets can be attacked and which safety/recovery routes cannot be cut off. Preserve previously earned cash in a documented custody location instead of accidentally deleting or duplicating it. | Break a gate, stop repair halfway, destroy a processor while it is loaded and restart with a buyer waiting; check pathfinding, power/routing, resources, and the Level 1 replacement. Ensure a damaged plot never charges more than the intended repair or silently repairs again on reload. |
| **P0 · Replaceable companions need a real life cycle.** | A killed pet/survivor is removed from active duty and must be bought again. Keep an ownership/recruitment history and unique active life IDs so reload cannot resurrect, duplicate or keep assigning a dead actor. Defeats must not force premium spend. Decide explicitly what carried goods and earned upgrades do on death, whether replacement is the same named identity or a successor, and how separately purchased entitlements are honored. | Lose the last helper while it holds paid goods; quit after the fatal hit; rebuild a useful roster through an attainable ordinary-resource route. Confirm one death, one cargo disposition and no duplicated unlock or payment. |

## Three less obvious failures the source audit exposed
1. **Private cargo can suppress restocking.** Existing conveyor demand subtracts every held meal from the customer queue, even if a player walks away with that meal. The belt also fills spare rack space without a demand check, and the manual bulk drop does not share that capacity gate. In the new loop, only specifically reserved stock counts as promised, including manual, worker and belt transfers. [Conveyor demand][belts] · [Player drop][exchange]
2. **Today’s save model cannot simply absorb larger baskets and prices.** It validates just the current Level 1/2 settlement, limits the worker to zero or one finished meal and caps cash by total sales. The new sale/order, actor-life and structure ledgers need versioned migration; keep earlier banked value and unlocks. Active orders need IDs independent of the served count and must not reroll on reload. [Settlement save validation][saves] · [Counter activation][queue]
3. **Expansion, collision and safety already share old assumptions.** The current encounter controller removes newly interior wolves when a perimeter changes; camp boundaries also directly suppress damage. A breach must change physical passage, navigation, protection and contact together, and the game must neither erase an active raid by expanding camp nor create rewards from reloading. Use a finite, warned active-session siege state independent of viewport. [Perimeter reconciliation][reconcile] · [Player damage][damage]

## Furnace: recommended consequence, not a locked design
When the furnace is destroyed, put the camp into a **cold emergency**. Suspend hearth healing and the services that actually depend on the hearth, pause normal customer demand there, and show an emergency rebuild plot. Do not tie unrelated warehouses or machines to hearth power without a reason. Retain paid land, secured stock and past progress; protect a minimal fallback spawn and at least one hand-gatherable route to the rebuild materials. No gems required. Recover the furnace at Level 1 under the equipment rule, then allow earned upgrades again. This makes the loss change play immediately without making it unrecoverable. Decide later whether a temporary collapse of local sanctuary is warranted; do not silently convert the fallback spawn into a siege target.

## AI architecture and decisions to freeze
Use local, deterministic game AI: a resource/prerequisite graph for feasibility; a bounded, scored job planner for workers; separate state machines for enemy intent and damage; and a shared durable transaction ledger. Evaluate on meaningful state changes and at a bounded cadence, not every actor against every option each frame. Add a simple replayable decision trace (“serving paused: cooker destroyed,” “worker chose warehouse: counter full,” “camp threatened: outside defense range”). This delivers context-aware play offline without cloud AI, unpredictable outputs or a model in the transaction path.

The current plan’s CD01/CD02 protect named actors, preserve a permanent sanctuary and specify free restoration of already-owned damage. The new directives deliberately reverse parts of that policy. Keep their non-conflicting protections (no absence losses or compulsory paid recovery) and explicitly supersede the affected rules and tests. Do not let a historical pass hide the conflict. The distance threshold, warning/grace time, starter difficulty, casualty upgrade/cargo fate and exact furnace penalty still need balancing. For launch, use distinct warning, damage, break, retry and recovery animations plus audio, and equivalent visual cues when muted. Judge at the real gameplay camera and test several approaches, not one ideal lap.

**Suggested task ownership:** T15/T17 customer behavior and settlement; T10/T11 purchase, upgrade and structure state; T12/T13/T33 feasibility, difficulty and free recovery; T21/T22 enemy/hunt contact and damage; T23–T31 owned actors and job decisions; T08/T14 shared custody and save transitions; T54/T55 audio and readable cues. This is an audit handoff, not an activation, scope unlock or repo change. First prove the complete bulk-counter loop and one recoverable camp-loss scenario on the same foundation before rolling the rules across regions.

## Aurora and controls

Implement matching Light/Dark tokens on the same sparse HUD. Keep readable resources, objectives, action controls, minimap and larger map across actual phone/tablet/foldable windows. A visible joystick is allowed as movement feedback, styled to match Aurora. Its location must not restrict where movement can begin. Keep feedback fully inside the actual safe bounds even for edge/corner touches; visual repositioning must not change the logical touch origin, direction or speed. The authored movement model allows dragging anywhere in the gameplay viewport not owned by UI, including over buildings and actors, without touching the displayed joystick first. Release or cancellation stops movement. Buttons and map gestures keep ownership; map pan/pinch must not move or buy underneath. ## Day/night population and aggression

Day can feature grazing/foraging stag and caribou, ground birds and squirrels, and visible shield/ranged patrols. Dusk increases forest-cover movement and introduces sapper/leader pressure. Night includes hare and porcupine opportunities, Frost Stalker encounters and more committed wolf/group pressure. Dawn gradually returns activity to daytime patterns. Overlap is useful: a common attainable meat path remains available in every phase. At an eligible World 1 stage, the day and night pools must contain actual different wildlife and enemy types; recoloring the sky alone fails.

Use saved encounter state with world, habitat, local-level, time and route eligibility. Randomness changes where suitable groups appear and what they do; it does not allow creatures to pop into view, spawn inside camp equipment, block gates or reroll rare rewards on reload. Every introduced group respects a tuned density budget. Existing individuals finish, disengage or leave naturally at phase changes. Never erase an actor still visible, hunted, reserved, wounded, engaged or holding an unsettled drop.

Night aggression is expressed through more decisive valid attacks, more persistent search/pursuit, group coordination, flanking and different encounter composition. Preserve detection/occlusion rules, committed attack timing, warning tells, recovery openings and attainable counterplay. Avoid omniscient pursuit, perfect tracking during a charge, unavoidable stun/tether loops and arbitrary instant damage. Night does not change survivor eligibility or turn neutral animals into enemies.

The ordinary world clock advances with active play and pauses while backgrounded/closed under the existing timing rule. Save the phase, encounter origin, RNG state and countdowns. Phone wall-clock edits, theme switching, map opening, rotation and reload cannot grant new spawns/rewards or erase danger. The expanded map must bind the actual modal pause policy before integration.

## Natural movement has two layers

**Behavior:** animals select useful feeding, cover, resting and escape destinations; herds maintain spacing and react with individual delays. Survivors keep useful jobs stable, look toward a destination, approach work faces, yield in narrow lanes and recover from a blocked route. Bounded rest/idle activity is valid when no authorized job exists. Adding random wandering while real work waits is not an improvement.

**Body motion:** each species needs its own gait, foot/hoof/paw contact, stride-speed match, body compression, weight transfer, turn radius and start/stop transitions. Humans need planted feet, toe roll/knee bend, believable load balance, correct grip and appropriate unloaded/carrying/tool-ready movement. Ears, heads, tails, wings and gear follow the motion without robotic synchronization or random joint noise.

The required motion set includes the applicable rest/feed/alert/walk/run/bound/fly/takeoff/land/turn/start/stop/flee/attack/miss/hit/interruption/defeat states and transitions. Survivor proof covers hunting and ordinary work: harvest, carry, stock, collect cash, fund plots, build, repair, guard and resume. Two-handed actions, mixed loads, gates, conveyors and other actors must remain compatible.

Actual review uses complete normal-speed and slow-motion sequences from gameplay, front/rear/sides/three-quarter and useful contact closeups. Check feet/toes/knees, paws/hooves, wing clearance, body/gear clipping, carry handling and damage contact. Listen to actual footsteps, calls, work and combat cues. Reject foot skating, spinning in place, snapped poses, hover contacts and cloned herd timing. A 360 still, single walk clip or schema pass cannot approve these behaviors.

## Findings that must reach the task plan

1. **Reference A contains a real connected production/service layout.** At 14–16s an intake and inclined belt feed the staffed whole-fish grill. At 38–42s a separate wheat-like chain produces bread-like forms. At 56.5–59s differentiated feeds enter an assembler and its output reaches a vehicle counter; green cash returns to a separate inside pad. The plan must preserve physical input, processing, output, service and payment stages, with concurrent manual carry.
2. **The sales catalogue is product-specific.** Primary B visibly sells raw logs and meat. A shows cooked food and bread-like products. A global finished-goods-only rule would conflict with the sources. A supplemental cutting-counter example is a different chain and does not turn A’s whole-fish cooking into a mandatory fillet recipe.
3. **The camp is composed around readable work.** The working floor remains open, customers wait outside, equipment and stock occupy readable edges, and the player moves through the same frame as the mechanisms. Wide static overview images and isolated asset views cannot establish this behavior. Landscape adaptation must retain scale and routes without stretching portrait reference footage.
4. **Carry volume is part of the presentation.** Tall stacks and distinct mixed types are visible; item travel and cash movement make the loop legible. Use the latest owner-approved two-row carry direction and unlimited logical inventory without inferring an unlimited number of rendered objects. Test clipped, fully loaded and interrupted transfers.
5. **The universal warehouse is an approved Havenline extension.** The footage establishes dedicated pads, trays, carry routes and some direct machine links. It does not prove universal warehouse sorting, every-item-via-storage routing, capacities, reserve rules or numerical throughput. Keep those choices explicit and test them against the preserved conveyor and storage renders.
6. **Weapon and topology conflicts require an explicit adaptation record.** B shows orbiting axes; the latest owner direction prefers two-handed harvesting. Both facts belong in the plan. The reference is not authority to undo later owner direction. Similarly, the latest small Level 1 and two usable bidirectional openings must not be overwritten by older full-map portal/river contracts.
7. **Enemy coverage is broader than three concepts.** Prior research separately identifies plain polar bear, spined white quadruped, red skull/beak-mask hood minion, larger matching boss, red winter rifle infantry, large rifle leader, Giant Elk and Horned Snow Beast. Tundra Stag is a separate hunting appearance. Keep canonical source-to-render mappings and their visual-role uncertainty; preserve the existing wolf/raider/Ice Brute concepts without claiming they cover every reference subject. YT13’s tiny red assault crowd remains an unresolved visual identity.
8. **Audio is unverified reference evidence.** Reference A is silent at the file level, and B has not been listened to in the recovered audit. Music and sound therefore need explicit creative decisions, event ownership and listening tests. T54 should integrate cues already owned by task-level mechanics rather than letting all audio remain a late empty bucket.

## How to assess the 9.9 target

Require a reference-pair package for each applicable scenario, all bound to one exact candidate SHA. Include source ID/hash and source window; explicit owner adaptations; approved asset SHA/path; exact runtime scene/state and progression; normal landscape camera plus diagnostic views; raw still/motion captures; source/asset/build hashes; critic findings and retest disposition.

The comparison must cover distinct dimensions: visual language/material finish; camera/composition; actor/object scale and occlusion; visible gameplay sequence; physical transfers and custody; animation/contact; UI/adaptation; sound when applicable; performance; persistence and evidence integrity. A good total score cannot average away a missing or failing dimension. “Matches 9.9” must never be inferred from a design render, a checklist, an old reviewer score or a fresh screenshot without motion. Preserve specialty gates at 10.0 and all mandatory checks.

For camera and layout, compare the same progression state and route before, during and after the action. Annotate normalized actor/machine/fence size, accessible pickup and service faces, and which objects hide each other. Do not invent a universal numeric tolerance without calibrated reference measurement. Match A’s shore-to-production scene and B’s early/expanded camp separately; they are distinct scenes, not one indiscriminate prop pile.

For production and transfers, prove source decrement/destination increment, visual travel, eligible material, finite buffers, full-destination hold, restart recovery and exactly-once payment. Include stock competing for manual carry and automation, empty inputs, missing ingredients, relocation/rotation and supported transfer surfaces. These are implementation tests needed to turn the ad presentation into a reliable game; the footage itself does not prove conservation or jam handling.

For motion, inspect complete gait/action cycles, transitions, turns, feet/toes/knees, hand/tool contact, loaded/unloaded posture and clipping at gameplay scale and useful close views. A storyboard frame cannot certify animation. Current owner reports of backwards-looking legs and fast survivor motion remain reopenings even when older isolated scores passed.

For audio, the minimum cue map should cover environment/region/weather; footsteps/gear; wood/stone/metal contact; fishing/acquisition; stack pickup/deposit; each machine’s start/run/stop/empty/full states; conveyors/transfers; gates; construction/upgrade; orders/queue/service/cash; enemies/damage/defense; rescue/companions; UI/menus and accessibility. Actual listening must check loop seams, event sync, concurrent sound density, ducking, interruption/resume, level transitions and mute settings. Source audio limitations are explicit; do not claim music imitation has been verified.
